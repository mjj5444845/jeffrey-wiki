**You Really Didn't Get That? Benchmarking Social Pragmatic Inference for Indirect and Playful Chinese Online Comments** is a research paper co-authored by [[Home|Junjie Ma]] and published in the proceedings of the EMNLP 2026 Main Conference. It investigates whether large language models can infer what a speaker means through indirect or playful comments in Chinese online conversations.[¹](#reference-1)

## Background

Recognizing sarcasm or humor does not necessarily establish whom a comment targets or what social purpose it serves. The study examines these distinctions in context, including expressions associated with *yinyang guaiqi* (阴阳怪气).[¹](#reference-1)

## Benchmark

The authors constructed 4,735 human-validated questions from public Chinese social media interactions. Eight language models proposed questions and answered those written by other models; human reviewers checked the interpretations and answer choices.[¹](#reference-1)

## Findings

The strongest model achieved 81.42% accuracy on questions written by other models. Human accuracy on a 300-item sample was 90.8%. The analysis found that models could recognize a playful tone while misinterpreting its target or function. These results concern the study's Chinese-language benchmark.[¹](#reference-1)
